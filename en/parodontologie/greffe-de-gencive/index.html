Periodontology

Gum grafts

Covering an exposed root and strength­en­ing gums that are too thin.

Gum recession

Gum recession is when the gum pulls back and exposes part of the root. The tooth looks longer, and the neck of the tooth often becomes sensitive.

Causes

  • Brushing too hardEspecially with a hard toothbrush or a side-to-side scrubbing motion.
  • Thin gumsSome people naturally have more fragile gums.
  • PeriodontitisBone loss causes the gum to recede.
  • Tooth positionA tooth that sits outside the dental arch, too far towards the lip, sometimes after orthodontic treatment.

When is a graft recommended?

  • An exposed root that is sensitive or visible when you smile
  • Recession that is getting worse
  • Gums that are too thin before orthodontic or prosthetic treatment

Techniques

Connective tissue graft
A small piece of tissue is taken from just beneath the surface of the palate (the roof of the mouth) and placed under the gum being treated. It is the standard technique for covering an exposed root.
Free gingival graft
A piece of gum tissue is taken from the surface of the palate and used to thicken the gum.

In some cases, a substitute material can be used instead of tissue from the palate. The choice depends on your situation.

Recovery

The procedure is done under local anaesthetic. The palate may stay tender for one to two weeks. Soft, lukewarm food is recommended for the first few days, and the grafted area must not be brushed for the period your dentist specifies.

The gum heals within a few weeks, and the final result can be judged after a few months.

Frequently asked questions

Will the root be fully covered?

It depends on the type of recession and the bone around the tooth. Your dentist will explain the expected result before the procedure.

Is a bone graft covered by health insurance?

Gum grafts are generally not covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie). Some top-up health insurance providers (mutuelles) contribute. You will be given a quote before treatment begins.
