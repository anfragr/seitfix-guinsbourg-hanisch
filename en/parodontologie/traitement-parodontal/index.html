Periodontology

Periodontal treatment

From assessment to root planing, then reassessment.

The periodontal assessment

Treatment starts with a full assessment: pocket depths are measured around each tooth, and X-rays show the bone level.

This assessment is used to determine the stage of the disease and to propose a treatment plan.

The stages of treatment

  1. 1Oral hygieneAdvice and suitable cleaning aids so that you can clean the affected areas every day.
  2. 2Root planing (deep cleaning)Under local anaesthetic, plaque and tartar are removed from below the gumline, from the root surfaces.
  3. 3ReassessmentA few weeks later, the pockets are measured again.
  4. 4Surgery, if necessaryWhere pockets persist, a targeted procedure may be suggested.

Root planing is done in one or more appointments, depending on how extensive the disease is.

After root planing

Teeth are often sensitive to cold for a few days to a few weeks. As the inflammation settles, the gums tighten: your teeth may look slightly longer and small gaps may appear between them.

What next?

Periodontitis is a chronic disease. Once it has been stabilised, it needs regular follow-up to stop it coming back. This is explained on the maintenance and hygiene page.

Frequently asked questions

How long does treatment take?

The active phase, from the initial assessment to the reassessment, usually takes a few months. Follow-up then continues over the long term.

Should I stop smoking?

Yes, this is strongly advised. Smoking makes treatment less effective and increases the risk of the disease coming back.
