Peri­od­on­to­logy

Treating the gums and bone that support your teeth, and keeping them healthy afterwards.

Book an appointment 01 45 74 66 56

What is peri­od­on­to­logy?

The periodontium is the name for the tissues that surround and support each tooth: the gum, the bone and the ligament that attaches the root to the bone.

Periodontology deals with diseases of these tissues. The most common is gingivitis, an inflammation of the gums. The most serious is periodontitis, which gradually destroys the tissues that hold the teeth in place. It often develops without pain, which is why it is frequently picked up late.

Risk factors

  • Smoking
  • Diabetes, especially when poorly controlled
  • Inadequate oral hygiene
  • A family history of periodontitis
  • Pregnancy, which makes gum inflammation more likely

Long-term follow-up

Treat, then maintain

Periodontal treatment aims to stop the inflammation. Regular maintenance appointments at the practice then help to keep your gums healthy and pick up any recurrence early.

Frequently asked questions

Can periodontitis be cured?

The inflammation can be stopped and the disease stabilised. Bone that has been lost does not generally grow back by itself, which is why early treatment and regular follow-up are important.

Is the treatment painful?

Root planing is done under local anaesthetic. Teeth are often sensitive to cold for a few days to a few weeks afterwards.

Is it covered by health insurance?

Apart from scaling and X-rays, periodontal treatment is not covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie), except for some patients with a long-term condition (ALD): diabetes and certain heart, immune or rheumatic diseases. Some top-up health insurance providers (mutuelles) contribute. You will be given a quote before treatment begins.

Bleeding gums?

A gum examination shows whether it is simple inflammation or periodontitis, so that it can be treated early.
