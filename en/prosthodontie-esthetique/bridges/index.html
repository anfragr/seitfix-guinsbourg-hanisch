Aesthetic prosthodontics

Bridges

Replacing one or more missing teeth using the neighbouring teeth for support.

What is a bridge?

A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth with artificial teeth, held in place by the teeth on either side.

It is a single fixed unit: the supporting teeth are crowned, and these crowns are joined to the replacement tooth or teeth.

Types of bridge

Conventional bridge
The teeth on either side are crowned and hold the replacement tooth between them.
Resin-bonded bridge
Most often used for a front tooth: the replacement tooth is attached by a small wing bonded to the back of one or both neighbouring teeth, with very little preparation.
Implant-supported bridge
It is supported by implants rather than natural teeth.

Bridge or implant?

A conventional bridge involves shaping the neighbouring teeth. That is an advantage if they are already damaged and need crowns anyway. If they are healthy, an implant means they can be left untouched.

After the examination, your dentist explains both options and their pros and cons in your particular case.

The procedure

  1. 1PreparationThe supporting teeth are shaped under local anaesthetic.
  2. 2ImpressionAn impression is taken so that the bridge can be custom-made.
  3. 3TemporaryYou wear a temporary bridge while the final one is being made.
  4. 4FittingThe final bridge is adjusted, then fixed in place.

Caring for your bridge

Food and plaque easily build up under the replacement tooth. Cleaning under the bridge every day with interdental brushes or a suitable floss protects the supporting teeth and the gums.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bridge covered by health insurance?

A bridge is partly covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie), and some bridges are part of the French 100 % Santé scheme, under which patients with a qualifying top-up health insurance (mutuelle) have nothing left to pay. The quote shows how much you will have to pay yourself.

How many teeth can a bridge replace?

Usually one or two. Beyond that, there is a risk of overloading the supporting teeth, and other options, such as implants, are often considered.
