Aesthetic pros­tho­dontics

Crowns, veneers, bridges and implant-supported prostheses that restore the shape, function and natural look of your teeth.

What is pros­tho­dontics?

Prosthodontics is the field of dentistry behind crowns, veneers and bridges. It restores damaged teeth and replaces missing ones.

Treatment can range from a single crown to restoring all the teeth in one jaw. Each restoration is designed to match the shape, shade and function of the teeth around it.

When should you bring your child in?

  • A badly damaged, fractured or root-filled tooth
  • One or more missing teeth
  • A tooth whose shade or shape bothers you
  • An old crown or bridge that needs replacing

Choosing the right restoration

Four options, each suited to a particular situation. You and your dentist choose together after the examination.

Crown

A cap that fully covers a damaged tooth.

All about crowns
Suitable for
A badly broken-down, fractured or root-filled tooth with a healthy root.
Tooth preparation
The tooth is shaped all the way round to make room for the crown.
Materials
Porcelain fused to metal, or all-ceramic materials such as zirconia or lithium disilicate.
Appointment
Usually two or three, with a temporary crown in the meantime.

Veneer

A thin ceramic shell bonded to the front surface of a tooth.

All about veneers
Suitable for
Front teeth with little damage whose shade, shape or minor flaws bother you.
Tooth preparation
Minimal: a few tenths of a millimetre of enamel, sometimes none.
Material
Usually ceramic.
Appointment
Several, including an aesthetic assessment and a mock-up tried in the mouth.

Bridge

One or more artificial teeth held in place by the neighbouring teeth.

All about bridges
Suitable for
One or more missing teeth, where the teeth on either side also need crowns.
Tooth preparation
The neighbouring teeth are shaped to support the bridge.
Materials
The same as for a crown.
Appointment
Usually two or three.

Implant-supported prosthesis

One or more replacement teeth fixed to implants.

All about implant-supported prostheses
Suitable for
One or more missing teeth, where implants can be placed.
Tooth preparation
None on the neighbouring teeth: the implants are placed first.
Materials
Ceramic or resin, on a titanium or zirconia framework.
Appointment
After the implants have healed, usually two to four.

Frequently asked questions

Is a ceramic crown strong?

Yes, provided the material is right for the tooth. Modern ceramics, zirconia in particular, withstand chewing forces, including on the molars (back teeth).

Is prosthetic treatment covered by health insurance?

Crowns and bridges are partly covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie). Depending on the material and the position of the tooth, some are part of the French 100 % Santé scheme, under which patients with a qualifying top-up health insurance (mutuelle) have nothing left to pay. The implants themselves are not reimbursed, and a crown on an implant is reimbursed only to a limited extent, outside the 100 % Santé scheme. Veneers are cosmetic and are not covered. You will receive a detailed quote before any prosthetic treatment begins.

How long does a crown last?

With good care, a crown usually lasts many years. Good oral hygiene and regular check-ups also protect the tooth underneath.
