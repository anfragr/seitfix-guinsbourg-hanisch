Implantology

The implant procedure

What happens before, during and after an implant is placed, and how to prepare.

Before the procedure

At your first appointments, the dentist examines your mouth, arranges a 3D scan and reviews your general health.

Tell us about any medication you are taking, especially anticoagulants (blood thinners) and osteoporosis treatment, and let us know if you have diabetes. If you smoke, cutting down or stopping a few weeks before the procedure improves healing.

The day before and on the day

  • Have a light meal before the appointment, unless instructed otherwise.
  • Take any prescribed medication as directed.
  • If you are prescribed a sedative, do not drive and arrange for someone to take you home.
  • Avoid smoking on the day of the procedure.

The day of the procedure

The procedure takes place in the practice’s operating room under local anaesthetic. In most cases, the dentist opens the gum, prepares a site in the bone, places the implant and closes the gum with a few stitches.

For a single implant, the procedure usually takes less than an hour. You stay awake, and the local anaesthetic means that, in principle, you feel no pain. Before going home, you can rest in the practice’s recovery room.

The first few days

  1. On the day

    Apply something cold to your cheek for ten minutes at a time. Do not rinse your mouth or spit. Eat soft food, lukewarm or cold, on the other side of your mouth.

  2. The next two to three days

    Swelling, sometimes with slight bruising, is normal and usually peaks on the second or third day. Take the prescribed medication and brush your other teeth as usual, avoiding the treated area for the first few days.

  3. The first week

    Avoid smoking, alcohol and strenuous exercise. Use the prescribed mouthwash, usually starting the day after the procedure.

  4. After seven to ten days

    A follow-up appointment is arranged to check healing and remove the stitches if needed.

This is general advice. Always follow the instructions your dentist gives you.

Call the practice if you notice

  • bleeding that does not stop after twenty minutes of applying pressure
  • a fever
  • swelling that gets worse after the third day
  • pain that is not relieved by the prescribed medication

Healing and the crown

The implant takes two to six months to fuse with the bone. In visible areas, a temporary solution (fixed or removable, depending on the case) is planned so that you are not left without a tooth.

Once the implant has integrated, the dentist takes an impression. The final crown is custom-made and then fitted over one or two appointments.

Follow-up

You brush an implant just like a natural tooth, and also use interdental brushes around the crown. Check-ups with a professional clean, at intervals set according to your situation and at least once a year, help to spot any inflammation early.

Frequently asked questions

Can I go back to work the next day?

After a single, straightforward implant, most patients return to light activities the next day. After a more extensive procedure, allow two to three days’ rest.

How many appointments are needed?

Usually four to six: consultation and imaging, placement, a healing check, then the impression and fitting of the crown. A bone graft adds further steps.

Can the implant be placed on the same day as the extraction?

In some cases, yes. This is known as immediate implant placement. Whether it is possible depends on the condition of the bone and gum, as assessed on the 3D scan.
