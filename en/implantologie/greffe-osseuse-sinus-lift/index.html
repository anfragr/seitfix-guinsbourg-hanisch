Implantology

Bone graft and sinus lift

If there is not enough bone to place an implant, it can be rebuilt. Here is when and how this is done.

Why there may not be enough bone

After a tooth is lost, the bone that supported it is no longer stimulated and gradually shrinks (resorbs), especially in the first few months.

Infection, periodontitis (gum disease) or wearing a removable denture for a long time can make this loss worse. At the back of the upper jaw, the height of the bone is also limited by the sinus, which sits just above the roots.

The 3D scan taken before treatment measures precisely how much bone is available and shows whether a graft is needed.

The bone graft

It involves adding bone, or a bone substitute, where bone is missing. The material may come from the patient, most often taken from another area of the mouth, or be a substitute of human, animal or synthetic origin. These substitutes are very commonly used today. A membrane usually protects the area while it heals.

  • At the same time as the implantWhen only a little bone is missing, it is added during implant placement. The treatment timeline changes little.
  • Before the implantWhen a lot of bone is missing, the graft is done first. The implant is placed once the bone has consolidated, usually four to six months later.

The sinus lift

The maxillary sinus is an air-filled space above the upper premolars and molars. When there is not enough bone height below the sinus, a sinus lift involves gently raising the membrane that lines the sinus and placing graft material beneath it. The extra bone height then allows the implant to be anchored.

  • Crestal approachWhen only a little bone is missing, the sinus is reached through the implant site itself. The implant is usually placed during the same appointment.
  • Lateral approachWhen more bone is missing, a small window is opened in the side of the bone. The implant is placed during the same appointment or after six to nine months of healing.

How much longer does it take?

SituationExtra time before the implant is placed
Graft at the same time as the implantNone, though healing may take a little longer
Sinus lift, crestal approachNone in most cases
Bone graft before the implantFour to six months
Sinus lift, lateral approach (two-stage)Six to nine months

These are approximate timescales, adjusted to each case during planning.

After the graft

Recovery is similar to that after implant placement, though swelling is often a little more pronounced. After a sinus lift, avoid blowing your nose hard and flying for about two weeks, and sneeze with your mouth open.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bone graft painful?

It is done under local anaesthetic. Swelling and discomfort are common over the following days and can be relieved with the prescribed medication.

Are there alternatives to a graft?

Sometimes. In some cases, shorter or angled implants can avoid the need for a graft. The dentist will explain the options after the 3D scan.

Is a bone graft covered by health insurance?

Bone grafts and sinus lifts are generally not covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie). Some top-up health insurance providers (mutuelles) contribute, and you will receive a detailed quote before treatment. If you are insured outside France, check with your insurer what it covers.
