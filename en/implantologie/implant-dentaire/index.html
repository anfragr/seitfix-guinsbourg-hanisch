Implantology

Dental implants

What an implant is, how it is made and what it can replace.

An artificial root

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth. It is placed in the jawbone, where it gradually fuses with the bone.

This process is called osseointegration. Once the implant has integrated, it holds a securely fixed artificial tooth without relying on the neighbouring teeth for support.

Three parts

The implant
A screw a few millimetres in diameter, placed in the bone. It replaces the root.
The abutment
The connecting piece that is fixed to the implant and passes through the gum.
The crown
The visible part, usually ceramic, custom-made to match the shade of the neighbouring teeth.

Titanium or ceramic

The vast majority of implants are made of titanium, a metal that is very well tolerated by the body and has been used in implantology for several decades.

Ceramic (zirconia) implants, which are white, are also available and are used in certain situations. The dentist will tell you whether this option is suitable for you.

What an implant can replace

  • A single toothOne implant and one crown, without touching the neighbouring teeth.
  • Several teethTwo implants can support a three-tooth bridge, sometimes four depending on the case.
  • All the teeth in one jawDepending on the case, four to six implants support a fixed full-arch prosthesis, or two to four implants stabilise a removable denture.

Implant, bridge or removable denture

There are three ways to replace a missing tooth. The dentist will help you choose based on the condition of your teeth and bone, and on what you expect.

How long does an implant last?

An implant that has integrated well and is properly looked after can last for many years. The crown itself may wear over time and need replacing at some point.

Two things matter: thorough daily oral hygiene and regular check-ups at the practice. If inflammation of the gum around the implant is not treated early, it can progress to peri-implantitis, with bone loss, and weaken the implant.

Frequently asked questions

Is an implant visible?

Generally not. Only the crown can be seen, and it is made to match the shade of your teeth.

Can you be allergic to titanium?

This is very rare. If you have ever had a metal allergy, mention it at the consultation so that other options can be discussed.

Can you chew just as you would with a natural tooth?

Yes, an implant lets you chew normally. It feels similar to a natural tooth, though not exactly the same.
