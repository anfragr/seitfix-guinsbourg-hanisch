Implantology

Replacing one or more missing teeth with implants.

Dr Oliver HanischMaster of Science in implantology (United States)

Book an appointment 01 45 74 66 56

What is a dental implant?

An implant is a small screw, usually made of titanium, that is placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth.

Once it has fused with the bone, it acts as a base for a crown, a bridge or a full-arch prosthesis. Unlike a conventional bridge, it does not require the neighbouring teeth to be filed down, so they are left intact.

When might an implant be an option?

  • A tooth is missing or needs to be extracted
  • Several adjacent teeth are missing
  • A removable denture does not stay in place properly
  • An existing bridge needs to be replaced

The stages of treatment

Without a bone graft, treatment usually takes three to six months from the first consultation to the final crown. If a graft is needed first, it can take a year or more.

  1. 1

    The consultation

    An examination of your mouth, a panoramic X-ray and a discussion of your expectations and medical history. The dentist explains the options available and gives you a detailed quote.

  2. 2

    3D imaging and planning

    A 3D scan measures the volume and quality of the bone. The implant position is then planned in advance from these images.

  3. 3

    Placing the implant

    The procedure is carried out in the operating room under local anaesthetic. For a single implant, it usually takes less than an hour.

  4. 4

    Healing

    Depending on the case, the implant takes two to six months to fuse with the bone. A temporary tooth can be worn in the meantime.

  5. 5

    Fitting the crown

    An impression is taken and the final crown is then fixed to the implant, in a shade chosen to match the neighbouring teeth.

  6. 6

    Follow-up

    As with natural teeth, regular check-ups and careful oral hygiene help the implant last longer.

Frequently asked questions

Does having an implant placed hurt?

The procedure is done under local anaesthetic, so in principle it is painless. Some discomfort or swelling is possible over the following days and can be relieved with the prescribed medication.

How long does an implant last?

With good care and regular check-ups, an implant can last for many years. Daily oral hygiene and follow-up at the practice matter as much as the placement itself.

Can anyone have an implant?

In most cases, yes, once growth is complete. Some situations call for preparation or particular precautions: insufficient bone, poorly controlled diabetes, certain osteoporosis treatments, or radiotherapy to the head and neck. In patients receiving bisphosphonates or denosumab for cancer, implants are, as a rule, contraindicated. Smoking increases the risk of failure. All of this is assessed at the consultation.

Are implants covered by health insurance?

The implant itself is generally not covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie). The crown fitted on it may be partly covered, and many top-up health insurance providers (mutuelles) contribute. You will receive a detailed quote before any treatment begins. If you are insured outside France, check with your insurer what it covers.
