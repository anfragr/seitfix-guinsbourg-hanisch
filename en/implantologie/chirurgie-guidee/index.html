Implantology

Guided surgery

Placing the implant with a custom-made guide designed from 3D imaging.

The principle

In guided surgery, the position of each implant is planned in advance on a computer, using 3D images of the jaw.

A custom surgical guide is then made: a template that fits over the teeth or gums, with holes drilled to match the planned position and angle. During the procedure, the instruments are passed through this guide.

The stages

  1. 13D imagingA 3D X-ray scan shows the bone, the path of the nerves and the sinus.
  2. 2The impressionAn impression, often taken digitally, records the shape of your teeth and gums.
  3. 3PlanningThe dentist chooses the size, position and angle of each implant, with the future crown already in mind.
  4. 4The guideThe custom guide is made and then used on the day of the procedure.

The benefits

  • PrecisionThe implant is placed as close as possible to the planned position, which helps to keep a safe distance from the nerves and the sinus.
  • An easier recoveryThe procedure is often shorter and, in some cases, can be carried out without opening the gum.
  • A well-positioned crownThe implant is positioned according to the tooth it will carry.

When is it used?

Guided surgery is particularly useful when several implants are being placed, when the bone is close to a nerve or the sinus, or in visible areas where the position of the crown is especially important.

For a single implant in good-quality bone, 3D planning without a guide may be enough. The dentist will tell you which approach is right for your case.

Frequently asked questions

Does guided surgery cost more?

Making the guide involves an extra cost, which is shown on the quote you receive before treatment.

Is the guide uncomfortable during the procedure?

Generally not. It is a template that sits over your teeth or gums during the procedure, which is carried out under local anaesthetic.
