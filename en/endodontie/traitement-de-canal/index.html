Root canal treatment

When it is needed, how it is carried out and what happens afterwards.

What does it involve?

Root canal treatment (known in French as dévitalisation) involves removing the inflamed or infected pulp, cleaning the inside of the tooth and then sealing it tightly.

The tooth is no longer alive, but it stays in place and continues to do its job when you chew.

When is it needed?

  • Deep decayDecay that has reached the pulp and caused inflammation, which is often painful.
  • An infection or abscessWhen the pulp has died and the infection has spread to the bone around the root.
  • TraumaA tooth that has been broken or knocked, where the pulp is exposed or does not survive.
  • Before a crown or bridgeA tooth sometimes needs root canal treatment before it can receive a crown or a bridge.

The procedure

  1. 1Anaesthesia and rubber damThe tooth is numbed, then isolated with a thin sheet called a rubber dam.
  2. 2Access and cleaningThe canals are opened, cleaned and disinfected.
  3. 3FillingThe canals are filled and sealed, in most cases with gutta-percha.
  4. 4RestorationThe tooth is sealed and then rebuilt, sometimes with a crown.

After the treatment

Your tooth will often feel tender when you bite for a few days. Standard painkillers recommended by your dentist can ease this.

Until the tooth has its permanent restoration, avoid chewing hard food on that side. A root-filled tooth that is not yet protected can fracture.

When root canal treatment is carried out properly and followed by a suitable restoration, the tooth can in most cases be kept in the long term. The prognosis depends on the condition of the tooth and is discussed with you before treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Why use a rubber dam?

The rubber dam isolates the tooth during treatment. It keeps saliva and the bacteria it carries out of the canals, and prevents small instruments from being swallowed or inhaled.

Is a crown always needed afterwards?

No. For a front tooth with little damage, a simple filling may be enough. For a molar or premolar, a crown is often recommended.
