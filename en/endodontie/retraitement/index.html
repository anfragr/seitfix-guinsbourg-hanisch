Endodontics

Retreatment

Redoing a previous root canal treatment when infection persists or comes back.

What is retreatment?

Retreatment means redoing a root canal treatment carried out previously, sometimes several years earlier, because infection has persisted or returned around the root.

The old root filling material is removed, and the canals are cleaned again and then resealed.

Why root canal treatment can fail

  • An untreated canalSome teeth have extra canals that are very fine or curved and hard to find without a microscope.
  • An incomplete fillingA canal that has not been filled all the way to its tip leaves room for bacteria.
  • LeakageA crown or filling that no longer seals properly allows bacteria from the mouth to get in.

Signs to look out for

Pain when biting, swelling or a small spot on the gum (a fistula, sometimes called a gum boil) can be a sign that a treatment has failed. Often there is no pain at all, and the problem is only found on a follow-up X-ray.

The procedure

  1. 1RemovalIf necessary, the crown or post is removed to access the canals.
  2. 2Removing the old materialThe old root filling is removed with precision.
  3. 3CleaningThe canals, including any that had been missed, are cleaned and disinfected.
  4. 4New fillingThe canals are resealed and the tooth is then restored.

Retreatment generally takes longer than a first treatment, often two appointments or more.

Alternatives

If retreatment is not possible or has not been enough, endodontic surgery can treat the infection at the tip of the root. As a last resort, the tooth is extracted and replaced, for example with an implant or a bridge.

Frequently asked questions

Is retreatment likely to succeed?

In many cases, yes. The chances depend on why the original treatment failed and on the condition of the tooth, both of which are assessed before starting.

Will the crown need replacing?

Often, yes, because the canals are reached through the crown. A crown that no longer sealed properly needs replacing in any case.
