Endodontics

Endodontic surgery

Treating the infection at the tip of the root when retreatment is not possible.

What does it involve?

Endodontic surgery, or apicectomy, treats an infection at the tip of the root by reaching it through the gum and bone.

Its aim, too, is to save the tooth.

When is it considered?

  • Retreatment is not possibleFor example, when a large post or a recent crown would make getting into the canals difficult or risky for the tooth.
  • Retreatment has not been enoughThe infection persists even though the canals have been properly treated.
  • Unusual anatomyA blocked or severely curved canal that cannot be treated along its full length.

The procedure

  1. 1Anaesthesia and accessUnder local anaesthetic, the gum is opened to reach the tip of the root.
  2. 2ResectionThe infected tissue and the last few millimetres of the root tip are removed.
  3. 3Root-end fillingThe end of the canal is sealed with a biocompatible material.
  4. 4StitchesThe gum is closed with a few stitches.

After the procedure

Swelling and discomfort are common for a few days. They can be eased with the medication prescribed and a cold compress held against the cheek. The stitches are removed a few days later, at a follow-up appointment.

Bone healing is monitored with follow-up X-rays over the following year, sometimes longer.

Frequently asked questions

Is the procedure painful?

Usually not, as it is done under local anaesthetic. Any discomfort afterwards is generally moderate.

Where does it take place?

At the practice, in a room set aside for surgery.
