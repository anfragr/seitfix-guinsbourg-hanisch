Paediatric dentistry

Dental care tailored to children, from their first tooth to their teenage years.

Dr Sophie de ScorraillePaediatric dentistry

Book an appointment 01 45 74 66 56

What is paediatric dentistry?

Paediatric dentistry is dental care for children and teenagers.

It takes into account a child’s growth, their baby teeth and the permanent teeth coming through, as well as each child’s age and personality. Before each step, time is taken to explain and show what will happen, so that the child feels confident.

When should you bring your child in?

  • For a first visit, at around the age of one
  • For a yearly check-up
  • If you notice a stain or a hole in a tooth, or if a tooth hurts
  • After a knock to a tooth
  • If your child still sucks their thumb or a dummy after the age of 3

Different needs at every age

Choose your child’s age.

The first teeth

The first teeth usually come through at around six months. Clean them from the moment they appear.

Brushing
Twice a day, done by an adult, with a small, soft toothbrush.
Toothpaste
1,000 ppm fluoride: just a smear, the size of a grain of rice.
At the practice
A first visit at around the age of one.
Best avoided
Letting your child fall asleep with a bottle of milk or a sugary drink.

All the baby teeth are in

By around the age of three, all twenty baby teeth have come through. Check-ups continue once a year.

Brushing
Twice a day: your child has a go first, then an adult finishes off.
Toothpaste
1,000 ppm fluoride, the size of a small pea.
At the practice
One check-up a year. From age 3 to 24, M’T dents, the French national health insurance’s preventive dental check-up, is offered every year.
Watch out for
Thumb-sucking or a dummy: best stopped around this age.

The permanent teeth come through

At around six, the first permanent molars come through behind the baby teeth, often unnoticed. The baby teeth gradually fall out.

Brushing
Twice a day for two minutes, still checked by an adult.
Toothpaste
1,000 to 1,450 ppm fluoride from age 6, as advised by your dentist.
At the practice
One check-up a year and, if needed, fissure sealants on the permanent molars.
Watch out for
How the teeth are lining up, to spot early on whether orthodontic treatment (a brace) may be needed.

The teenage years

The second molars come through at around twelve. Braces, sport and diet need particular attention.

Brushing
Twice a day, using interdental brushes if your child wears a brace.
Toothpaste
1,000 to 1,450 ppm fluoride, as advised by your dentist.
At the practice
One check-up a year and, if needed, fissure sealants on the second molars.
Watch out for
Sugary or acidic drinks, and a mouthguard for contact sports.

Frequently asked questions

At what age should my child first see a dentist?

At around the age of one, or in the months after the first teeth appear. An early visit is mainly a chance to advise parents and help the child get used to the dentist.

Do baby teeth need treatment?

Yes. A cavity in a baby tooth can hurt, become infected and damage the permanent tooth forming beneath it. Baby teeth also hold the space for the permanent teeth.

Is children’s dental care covered by health insurance?

Children’s dental care is covered by the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie). Every year from age 3 to 24, the M’T dents preventive check-up is covered in full by the Assurance maladie together with top-up health insurance (mutuelle), and there is nothing to pay upfront. These arrangements apply to children covered by the French health system.
