Paediatric dentistry

Prevention

Brushing, toothpaste, diet and fissure sealants.

Brushing by age

AgeFluoride toothpasteAmount
From the first tooth1,000 ppmA smear, the size of a grain of rice
From 3 to 6 years1,000 ppmThe size of a small pea
From 6 years1,000 to 1,450 ppm, as advised by your dentistThe size of a small pea

Teeth should be brushed twice a day, morning and evening. Until your child can manage alone, an adult helps or checks. After brushing, it is best to spit out the excess without rinsing heavily, so the fluoride can keep working.

Diet

  • Limit snacking and sugary drinks between meals
  • Offer water to drink
  • No bottle of milk or sugary drink at bedtime
  • After eating something sugary, a glass of water helps rinse the mouth

Fissure sealants

The permanent molars have deep grooves (fissures) where plaque builds up. Sealing means coating them with a thin layer of protective resin. It is quick and painless, and no anaesthetic is needed.

Whether it is needed depends on your child’s risk of tooth decay, which the dentist assesses. Ideally it is done soon after the first permanent molars come through, at around six, and then the second molars, at around twelve.

The M’T dents check-up

Every year from age 3 to 24, the French national health insurance (Assurance maladie) offers a preventive dental check-up called M’T dents. It is covered in full by the Assurance maladie together with top-up health insurance (mutuelle), with nothing to pay upfront. Any treatment found to be needed is covered on the same terms if carried out within six months, except prosthetic work and orthodontics. These arrangements apply to children covered by the French health system.

Frequently asked questions

Should you rinse after brushing?

It is best to spit out the excess toothpaste without rinsing heavily, so that the fluoride stays in contact with the teeth.

Are fissure sealants covered?

Fissure sealants on the first and second permanent molars are covered by the Assurance maladie until the child’s 16th birthday, once per tooth.
