Paediatric dentistry

Advice for parents

Dummies, teething, knocks to the teeth and sport.

Dummies and thumb-sucking

Thumb-sucking or using a dummy is normal in toddlers. If the habit carries on beyond the age of about three, it can change the shape of the palate (the roof of the mouth) and push the teeth out of position. Stopping gradually, with encouragement rather than telling-off, generally works better.

Teething

Teething can make a child grizzly, dribble more and want to bite on things. A chilled teething ring can help soothe them.

A high fever or severe diarrhoea should not be put down to teething: see your doctor.

After a knock to a tooth

A permanent tooth has been knocked out

  • Find the tooth and hold it by the crown, without touching the root
  • If it is dirty, rinse it for a few seconds in cold water, without rubbing
  • If you can, put it back into its socket and have your child bite gently on a tissue
  • Otherwise, keep it in milk, or failing that in saline solution, never in water
  • Call the practice straight away or, outside opening hours, the out-of-hours dental service: every minute counts

01 45 74 66 56

A baby tooth that has been knocked out should not be put back, as it could damage the permanent tooth. You should still bring your child in, to check for any other injury.

After any knock, even without a broken tooth, a check-up is recommended: a tooth can change colour weeks later.

Sport

For contact sports and sports with a risk of falls, such as rugby, boxing, hockey or roller skating, a mouthguard reduces the risk of dental injury. A custom-made mouthguard, made from an impression of the teeth, is generally better tolerated.

When should you think about orthodontics?

Checking how the teeth line up and how the jaws are positioned at the yearly check-ups helps to spot early any problems that benefit from being treated while the child is still growing. If needed, your dentist will refer you to an orthodontist.

Frequently asked questions

My child grinds their teeth at night. Is that a concern?

It is common in children and often passes. Mention it at the next check-up, so the dentist can look for signs of wear on the teeth.
