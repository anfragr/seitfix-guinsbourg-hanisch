Paediatric dentistry

Caring for baby teeth

Why baby teeth matter, and how they are treated.

Why they matter

  • EatingThey let your child chew properly.
  • SpeakingThey play a part in learning to speak clearly.
  • Holding the spaceThey guide the permanent teeth as they come through.
  • GrowingThey support the growth of the jaws.

The timeline

StageApproximate age
First toothAround 6 months
All twenty baby teeth in placeAround 2½ to 3 years
First permanent molarsAround 6 years
Last baby teeth fall outAround 11 to 12 years

These ages vary from child to child.

Tooth decay in young children

Baby teeth have thinner enamel and decay quickly. The main causes are snacking, sugary drinks and a bottle at bedtime.

An untreated cavity can be painful, become infected and damage the permanent tooth forming beneath it.

Possible treatments

Filling
A shallow cavity is cleaned out and the tooth is filled.
Preformed crown
A preformed crown protects a badly damaged baby tooth until it falls out.
Pulpotomy
Treating the nerve (pulp) of an affected baby tooth so that it can be kept.
Extraction and space maintainer
If the tooth cannot be saved, a small appliance holds the space for the permanent tooth.

Treatment is adapted to the child’s age and can be split into several short appointments.

Frequently asked questions

My child is scared. What can I do?

Talk to your dentist before the appointment. Treatment can be split into several short appointments, starting with the simplest steps.

Does a decayed baby tooth need treating if it is going to fall out anyway?

It depends on how long the tooth has left before it falls out and how far the decay has spread. Your dentist will advise you on a case-by-case basis.
